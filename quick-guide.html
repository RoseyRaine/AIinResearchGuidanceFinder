Quick decision guide

Can I use AI for this task?

Use these categories as a starting point. Always check discipline-specific expectations and any requirements from your supervisor, ethics approval, publisher, funder, contract or data management plan.

✓

Generally acceptable

Examples

  • Learning about a method
  • Brainstorming possible angles
  • Improving clarity of researcher-authored text
  • Generating administrative drafts
  • Creating dummy examples
  • Formatting code written by the researcher

Conditions

  • Use critical judgement.
  • Do not input restricted data.
  • Check outputs.
  • Declare the use if it materially shapes research or writing.
!

Use with caution

Examples

  • Drafting thesis sections
  • Generating literature review summaries
  • Suggesting themes from de-identified data
  • Translating research material
  • Creating diagrams or images
  • Coding or statistical assistance
  • Grant or manuscript drafting

Conditions

  • Discuss with your supervisor or project lead.
  • Check ethics, consent, copyright, data security and publisher or funder rules.
  • Keep an AI-use log.
  • Independently verify outputs.
  • Declare material use.
⚑

Explicit approval likely required

Examples

  • Interview transcripts or recordings
  • Raw survey data
  • Identifiable participant data
  • Confidential manuscripts
  • Unpublished theses
  • Peer-review material
  • Commercial-in-confidence data
  • Restricted cultural knowledge
  • Third-party copyrighted PDFs in public AI tools

Conditions

  • Do not proceed unless the use is authorised.
  • Check ethics, consent, contracts, data governance, copyright and intellectual property requirements.
  • Use an approved secure tool where permitted.
✕

Not acceptable

Examples

  • Inventing data
  • Fabricating references
  • Using AI to impersonate participants
  • Creating fake evidence
  • Concealing AI-generated text as entirely human-authored
  • Bypassing assessment or examination conditions
  • Uploading confidential examination material to public tools

Why not

  • May constitute research misconduct or academic misconduct.
  • May breach confidentiality, copyright or privacy law.
Remember: this table is a starting point, not formal ethics, privacy, copyright, security or governance approval. Read Section 5 in the full guidance.
