Quick decision guide

Can I use AI for this task?

Use these categories as a starting point. Always check discipline-specific norms and any specific requirements from your supervisor, ethics approval, journal or funder.

✓Generally acceptable8 examples
  • Learning about a research method or conceptUse critical judgement and verify important information against authoritative sources. Do not enter restricted or sensitive material into a public/consumer AI tool.
  • Exploring research ideas, questions, concepts or possible approachesUse AI to support thinking, not replace scholarly judgement. Declare use if it materially influences the research direction or output.
  • Supporting search strategy development and preliminary literature discoveryAI may be used to suggest keywords, synonyms, related concepts, subject headings, search combinations or possible sources. Researchers should verify search terms and references independently and use appropriate scholarly databases and established search methods for the final search.
  • Identifying possible research collaborators, networks or related research areasVerify names, affiliations, expertise and current roles independently before relying on the information.
  • Improving clarity, grammar or expression in researcher-authored textCheck that meaning, technical terminology, citations and scholarly voice have not been altered. Declare use where required by the thesis, publisher, funder or other relevant requirements.
  • Drafting routine administrative or research-support material, such as meeting agendas, plain-language notes or project communicationsDo not include confidential, personal, sensitive or restricted information in public/consumer AI tools. Check outputs before use.
  • Creating synthetic or dummy examples for learning, testing or demonstrating a methodClearly identify synthetic material as such and do not present it as real research data.
  • Formatting or explaining researcher-written codeVerify the code independently and test it before use. Do not upload confidential datasets or code containing sensitive information into public/consumer AI tools.
!Use with caution8 examples
  • Suggesting an outline, structure or headings for a thesis, article, report or other research writingUse suggestions as a scaffold only. The researcher must determine the argument, interpretation and final structure. Discuss significant use with a supervisor or project lead where appropriate.
  • Editing or substantially revising research writingCheck that the AI has not altered meaning, evidence, argument, citations or disciplinary terminology. Maintain the researcher’s scholarly voice and declare material use.
  • Generating summaries of literatureVerify summaries against the original sources. AI-generated summaries should not replace reading, critical appraisal or evidence synthesis.
  • Suggesting themes, categories or coding approaches from de-identified dataCheck the relevant ethics approval, consent, data management plan and tool conditions. De-identification does not automatically make data safe to upload. Human interpretation remains essential.
  • Translating research materialCheck accuracy, disciplinary terminology, cultural meaning and consent or confidentiality requirements. Human review may be required, particularly for participant material or culturally sensitive content.
  • Creating diagrams, images or other multimodal research outputsCheck accuracy, copyright, intellectual property, bias and publisher requirements. Clearly disclose AI-generated content where required.
  • AI-assisted analysis (including coding and interpretation) of research data, including use of embedded, University-approved or self-hosted AI toolsConfirm that the data may be processed using that tool or environment and that the use is consistent with ethics approval, consent, data governance, contracts and the data management plan. Validate the analytical method and outputs, retain human oversight, record the tool/model/version and relevant settings, and disclose material AI use as part of the research method.
  • Drafting or revising grant applications, manuscripts or other research outputsCheck funder, publisher, confidentiality, copyright and disclosure requirements. The researcher remains responsible for all submitted content.
⚑Usually not permitted without express approval6 examples
  • Inputting interview transcripts, recordings, survey responses, identifiable participant data or other human research data into a public/consumer AI toolDo not proceed unless the relevant ethics approval, participant consent, data governance requirements and tool conditions permit the use. Where AI-assisted processing is approved, use an appropriate University-approved or protected tool.
  • Inputting confidential manuscripts, unpublished theses, grant applications or peer-review material into a public/consumer AI toolProtect confidentiality and intellectual property. Only use an AI tool where the relevant process explicitly permits it and the tool provides appropriate protections.
  • Inputting commercial-in-confidence, partner-owned or contractually restricted information into an AI toolCheck contractual, confidentiality, data governance and intellectual property requirements before proceeding. Use only an approved tool where permitted.
  • Inputting Indigenous knowledges, culturally restricted information or unpublished community material into an AI toolDo not proceed without appropriate cultural governance, community permissions, ethics approval and data sovereignty considerations.
  • Uploading third-party copyrighted articles, books, PDFs or other protected material where the licence or terms do not permit AI processingCheck copyright, licence and platform terms. Access to material does not automatically provide permission to upload it to an AI system.
  • Using AI to produce the initial substantive draft of HDR or Honours research writing where this replaces the researcher’s own intellectual workDiscuss proposed use with the supervisor and check relevant candidature, assessment and research requirements. AI should not substitute for development of the researcher’s own argument, analysis and scholarly voice.
✕Not acceptable6 examples
  • Fabricating research data or evidenceThis may constitute a breach of research integrity or research misconduct.
  • Inventing or knowingly using fabricated references, quotations, statistics or findingsThis may constitute a breach of research integrity or research misconduct.
  • Using AI to impersonate research participants or create false participant responses without an explicitly approved methodological basisDo not represent synthetic or AI-generated material as genuine participant data.
  • Concealing material AI-generated content where disclosure is requiredMay breach research integrity, academic integrity, publisher, funder or institutional requirements.
  • Using AI to bypass assessment, examination, authorship or other requirementsMay constitute academic misconduct, research misconduct or breach of relevant conditions.
  • Uploading confidential examination or review material into a public/consumer AI toolMay breach confidentiality, privacy, copyright, contractual or research integrity obligations.
